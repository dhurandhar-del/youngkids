# Safety, Ethics, Privacy and Regulatory Requirements for a Kids (6–8) AI Voice and Vision Demo (as of 20 Nov 2026)

> Scope: "Young Leaders" live demo. One child aged 6–8 talks to an AI character on a laptop. An adult enters the child's first name and class and the parents' names and occupations. Audio and periodic webcam snapshots stream to the OpenAI Realtime API. A facilitator is present. The company is most likely based in India, and demos may also run elsewhere.
> These are research notes, **not legal advice**. "MUST" means a binding obligation (law or contract or vendor policy). "SHOULD" means recommended best practice. All pages were accessed 20 Nov 2026 unless another date is given. Where a page could not be fetched directly, this is flagged.

## 1. OpenAI policies: Under-18 API guidance, ZDR / Modified Abuse Monitoring, Realtime eligibility, retention, training, usage policies

### Takeaway
OpenAI's Under-18 guidance says you "should not" process personal data of children under 13 (or under the local age of digital consent, which is 18 in India) "without first implementing zero data retention". It also requires "reasonable steps" for age-appropriate content: disclosures, content filters, monitoring and escalation. OpenAI can audit and suspend. ZDR needs prior approval from OpenAI through sales. `/v1/realtime` is listed as ZDR-eligible with no limitations. Without ZDR, Realtime content is kept in abuse-monitoring logs for 30 days. API data is not used for training by default. Realtime has no India data residency (US and EU only).

### Inferences
- **Treat ZDR as a MUST, not a "should".** The guidance says "should not … without first implementing zero data retention", but it sits in a policy enforced by audit, suspension and termination. India's age of digital consent under DPDP is 18 (see §3), so every child in an Indian demo triggers the ZDR expectation. A 6–8-year-old is under 13 everywhere.
- **No ZDR approval means no real children.** Without ZDR, each child's voice, face snapshots, first name and parents' names sit in OpenAI abuse-monitoring logs for up to 30 days. Approval is discretionary ("prior approval by OpenAI") and goes through sales, so start early. The approval lead time is not published.
- Modified Abuse Monitoring also removes customer content from logs (images and files excepted "in rare cases"). However, the Under-18 guidance names **ZDR** specifically. Request ZDR. A ZDR-only project for the demo is the cleanest setup.
- `/v1/realtime/client_secrets` and `/v1/realtime/calls` (WebRTC) are not separate rows in the table. They are sub-paths of `/v1/realtime`, which is listed as ZDR-eligible. **Get written confirmation from OpenAI** that the client-secret session config (which will contain the child's name in the instructions) and WebRTC calls fall under ZDR. The **unified interface** (your server proxies SDP) keeps the standard key and the session config server-side. Either way, avoid putting surnames or parents' occupations into the session instructions.
- Turn off Agents SDK tracing and any session `store`/recording features for this project.
- India data residency is **not** available for Realtime, so Indian children's audio and images will be processed in the US or EU. DPDP s.16 permits this unless the government blacklists a country (see §3). Disclose it in the consent form.
- Set `OpenAI-Safety-Identifier` to a random per-session ID, not the child's name. This supports OpenAI abuse attribution without sending identity.
- Use the current flagship realtime model from the list above, per the "most current flagship models" best practice.

### Takeaway

## 3. India: DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

### Takeaway
A "child" under the DPDP Act is anyone under **18**. Section 9 requires **verifiable parental consent** before processing a child's data. It bans processing likely to cause a "detrimental effect on the well-being of a child" and bans "tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at children". The Rules (G.S.R. 846(E), 13 Nov 2025) bring the substantive provisions, including s.9 and Rule 10 verifiable consent, into force **18 months after publication, around 13–14 May 2027**. **As of Nov 2026 these duties are not yet legally in force**, but they become mandatory within months and are the right baseline now. The educational-institution exemption is narrow (tracking and behavioural monitoring only) and is unlikely to cover a commercial demo.

### Cited Findings
**Act text (DPDP Act 2023, Gazette 11 Aug 2023)** — [MeitY PDF of the Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- s.2(f): "'child' means an individual who has not completed the age of eighteen years." — [Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- s.9(1): "The Data Fiduciary shall, before processing any personal data of a child or a person with disability who has a lawful guardian obtain verifiable consent of the parent of such child or the lawful guardian, as the case may be, in such manner as may be prescribed." — [Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- s.9(2): "A Data Fiduciary shall not undertake such processing of personal data that is likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being of a child." — [Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- s.9(3): "A Data Fiduciary shall not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at children." — [Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- s.9(4): s.9(1) and 9(3) do not apply to prescribed classes or purposes. s.9(5) lets the government lower the age for "verifiably safe" fiduciaries. — [Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- s.8(2): "A Data Fiduciary may engage, appoint, use or otherwise involve a Data Processor to process personal data on its behalf … only under a valid contract." s.8(1) keeps the fiduciary responsible for processing done "by it or on its behalf by a Data Processor." — [Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- s.16(1): "The Central Government may, by notification, restrict the transfer of personal data by a Data Fiduciary for processing to such country or territory outside India as may be so notified." This is a negative-list model. — [Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- Penalty: the Schedule lists "Breach in observance of additional obligations in relation to children under section 9" as a penalty item. — [Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf). Secondary sources put the maximum at **up to ₹200 crore**. — [Veritect playbook](https://veritect.ai/digital-data-ai-law/dpdp-childrens-data-compliance-playbook) (*the amount column could not be machine-extracted from the gazette PDF, so verify it*)

**Rules text (DPDP Rules 2025, G.S.R. 846(E), notified 13 Nov 2025)** — [Official e-Gazette](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/267650.pdf); [English copy](https://dpdpa.com/DPDP_Rules_2025_English_only.pdf); [MeitY page](https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa?pageTitle=Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Rules-2025)
- **Commencement (Rule 1, verbatim):** "(2) Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 shall come into force on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette. (3) Rule 4 shall come into force one year after the date of publication of this Gazette. (4) Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 shall come into force eighteen months after the date of publication of this Gazette." — [Rules](https://dpdpa.com/DPDP_Rules_2025_English_only.pdf)
- Commencement of the Act's provisions: 14 Nov 2025 (Board, definitions); 14 Nov 2026 (Consent Manager registration); **14 May 2027** for the substantive provisions, "including notice and consent, breach reporting, reasonable security safeguards, verifiable consent for children's … data, … Data Principal rights, cross-border transfers … and repeal of Section 43A of the IT Act." — [AMS Shardul, 21 Nov 2025](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/). Lexology also reports dates of 14 Nov 2025, 14 Nov 2026 and 14 May 2027, while other sources say 13 May 2027. — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=314d0a35-26eb-45ef-b4df-5ee32f7ba127)
- **Rule 10(1) (verbatim):** "A Data Fiduciary shall adopt appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure that verifiable consent of the parent is obtained before the processing of any personal data of a child and shall observe due diligence, for checking that the individual identifying herself as the parent is an adult who is identifiable if required in connection with compliance with any law for the time being in force in India, by reference to (a) reliable details of identity and age of the individual available with the Data Fiduciary; or (b) details of identity and age, voluntarily provided (i) by the individual; or (ii) through a virtual token mapped to such details, which is issued by an authorised entity." An "authorised entity" includes one entrusted by law or government with issuing identity and age details or tokens, and "also includes details of identity and age or token made available and verified by a Digital Locker Service Provider" (i.e., DigiLocker). — [Rules](https://dpdpa.com/DPDP_Rules_2025_English_only.pdf)
- **Rule 3 notice (verbatim, minimum content):** the notice must "(a) be presented and be understandable independently of any other information …; (b) give, in clear and plain language, a fair account of the details necessary to enable the Data Principal to give specific and informed consent …, which shall include, at the minimum, (i) an itemised description of such personal data; and (ii) the specified purpose or purposes of, and specific description of the goods or services to be provided or uses to be enabled by, such processing; and (c) give, the particular communication link for accessing the website or app … and a description of other means … using which such Data Principal may (i) withdraw her consent, with the ease of doing so being comparable to that with which such consent was given; (ii) exercise her rights under the Act; and (iii) make a complaint to the Board." — [Rules](https://dpdpa.com/DPDP_Rules_2025_English_only.pdf)
- **Rule 6 security (excerpt):** minimum safeguards include "securing of personal data through encryption, obfuscation, masking or the use of virtual tokens", access control, "visibility on the accessing of such personal data, through appropriate logs", backups, and that the fiduciary "retain such logs and personal data for a period of one year" for detecting and investigating unauthorised access. It also requires "appropriate provision in the contract … between such Data Fiduciary and such a Data Processor … for taking reasonable security safeguards." — [Rules](https://dpdpa.com/DPDP_Rules_2025_English_only.pdf)
- **Rule 12 + Fourth Schedule (exemptions from s.9(1) and 9(3)), Part A item 3 (verbatim):** "A Data Fiduciary who is an educational institution. | Processing is restricted to tracking and behavioural monitoring (a) for the educational activities of such institution; or (b) in the interests of safety of children enrolled with such institution." Other Part A classes cover healthcare, crèches and school transport (location only). Part B purposes include "ensuring that any information, service or advertisement likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being of a child is not accessible to her" and age-confirmation and due-diligence under Rule 10. — [Rules](https://dpdpa.com/DPDP_Rules_2025_English_only.pdf)
- Law-firm reading: the education exemption is "limited", covering tracking and monitoring "required strictly for the educational activities of the institution" or "the interests of the safety of children", with "no exemption for edtech platforms or other third-party services". — [S.S. Rana & Co., 17 Jul 2026](https://ssrana.in/articles/schools-coaching-centres-platforms-processing-childrens-data-are-you-compliant/)
- Commentary notes it is untested whether coaching centres count as "educational institutions", and advises defaulting to consent. — [search summary of DPDP guides](https://www.codesnag.ai/blogs/dpdp-childrens-data-verifiable-parental-consent-schools-edtech) (secondary)

### Inferences
- **Roles.** Young Leaders decides the purpose and means, so it is the **Data Fiduciary**. OpenAI is a **Data Processor** and needs a "valid contract" (the OpenAI DPA) under s.8(2). Young Leaders stays liable for OpenAI's processing.
- **Personal data in scope:** the child's first name, class, voice audio and face images; the parents' names and occupations. Parents' data is adult personal data and needs ordinary s.6 consent, which they give on the same form.
- **Timing nuance.** As of 20 Nov 2026, s.9 and Rule 10 are not yet in force (due ~13/14 May 2027). Per AMS Shardul, the IT Act s.43A regime (and by implication the 2011 SPDI Rules) remains until then. Even so, design to the DPDP standard now. Running a children's AI demo without parental consent would be indefensible on reputation and ethics grounds, and the hard deadline is about six months away.
- **"Tracking or behavioural monitoring".** An AI that "comments on what it sees" in live snapshots, without building any profile, retaining anything, or influencing behaviour beyond the session, is arguably not "behavioural monitoring". Logging or analysing a child's behaviour, attention, emotions or engagement across sessions would carry a high risk of crossing that line. Do not store per-child engagement analytics. Do not run ads or attach persistent identifiers.
- **Education exemption.** It covers only tracking and monitoring by an "educational institution" for its own educational activities or child safety. It does not waive consent for a commercial AI demo, and the s.9(2) well-being duty has no exemption. Do not rely on it.
- **Verifiable consent in practice for a live demo.** The parent attends in person or signs in advance. The facilitator checks the parent's identity and age (Rule 10(1)(a)/(b)(i)), for example by sighting a government ID without copying it, or by using a DigiLocker token for online pre-registration. Record that the check was done, not the ID number.
- **Rule 6 tension.** Keep one-year **access logs and metadata** (who ran which session, and when). Do not keep children's audio, images or transcripts. That way the log-retention duty does not force you to retain child content. Confirm with counsel.
- **Cross-border.** Realtime has no India residency, so data goes to the US or EU. This is lawful under s.16 unless those countries are notified as restricted, which I found no sign of. Disclose it.

### Gaps
- Some secondary sources report that MeitY consulted in Jan 2026 on compressing timelines (e.g., to 12 months for Significant Data Fiduciaries). I found **no notified amendment**, so treat it as unverified, and re-check MeitY before launch.
- I could not extract the "educational institution" definition note in the Fourth Schedule cleanly (it reads "including vocational education"). Verify against the gazette.
- No DPB guidance yet on what counts as "behavioural monitoring" for conversational AI or webcams.
- The Penalty Schedule amount (₹200 crore) is confirmed only by secondary sources in this pass.
- The SPDI Rules 2011 (IT Act s.43A), which apply until May 2027, were not researched in detail. Whether face images count as "biometric" SPDI is unconfirmed.

## 4. US COPPA (amended 2025): reference for US demos

### Takeaway
Under COPPA a "child" is under 13. A photo, video or audio file containing a child's image or voice is personal information, and the 2025 amendments added biometric identifiers. Verifiable parental consent is required before collection. **The audio-file exception does not fit this product**, because it requires collecting "no other personal information" (here the product also captures webcam images and the child's name). The amended rule took effect 23 Jun 2025, and the compliance deadline was **22 Apr 2026**, so it is fully in force now.

### Cited Findings
- Dates: the amendments were published 22 Apr 2025, effective 23 Jun 2025, with compliance by 22 Apr 2026 for most provisions. — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-publishes-final-coppa-rule-amendments); [BBB National Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media/insights/blog/coppa-amended)
- "Child means an individual under the age of 13." — [16 CFR 312.2 (eCFR, current)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312)
- **Personal information (verbatim excerpts):** "(1) A first and last name; … (7) A persistent identifier … (8) A photograph, video, or audio file where such file contains a child's image or voice; … (10) A biometric identifier that can be used for the automated or semi-automated recognition of an individual, such as fingerprints; handprints; retina patterns; iris patterns; genetic data, including a DNA sequence; voiceprints; gait patterns; facial templates; or faceprints; or (11) Information concerning the child or the parents of that child that the operator collects online from the child and combines with an identifier described in this definition." — [16 CFR 312.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312)
- **Consent:** "An operator is required to obtain verifiable parental consent before any collection, use, or disclosure of personal information from children…" (312.5(a)(1)). The operator must offer separate consent to third-party disclosure "unless such disclosure is integral to the website or online service" (312.5(a)(2)). Accepted methods include "a consent form to be signed by the parent and returned … by postal mail, facsimile, or electronic scan" (312.5(b)(2)(i)), among others. — [16 CFR 312.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312)
- **Audio-file exception (verbatim, 312.5(c)(9)):** "Where an operator collects an audio file containing a child's voice, and no other personal information, for use in responding to a child's specific request and where the operator does not use such information for any other purpose, does not disclose it, and deletes it immediately after responding to the child's request. In such case, there also shall be no obligation to provide a direct notice, but notice shall be required under § 312.4(d)." — [16 CFR 312.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312)
- "Disclose" excludes provision "to a person who provides support for the internal operations of the website or online service." — [16 CFR 312.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312)
- **Retention (312.10, verbatim excerpts):** retain only "as long as is reasonably necessary to fulfill the specific purpose(s)"; "may not be retained indefinitely"; operator "must establish, implement, and maintain a written data retention policy" stating purposes, business need and "a timeframe for deletion", and must publish it in the online notice. — [16 CFR 312.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312)
- **Security (312.8(b)):** "must establish, implement, and maintain a written information security program", designate a coordinator, and run risk assessments at least annually. — [16 CFR 312.8](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312)
- The FTC declined to add biometric exceptions for security or age verification. — [search summary of Hunton/Loeb analyses](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/childrens-online-privacy-in-2025-the-amended-coppa-rule)

### Inferences
- **First name alone** is not on the list (the rule lists "first and last name"). In this product, though, the first name sits alongside voice and image, which are PI in their own right. Treat the whole session as PI.
- COPPA covers information "collected online **from a child**". Parents' names and occupations typed in by an adult are not collected from the child. The child's voice and image are, so COPPA applies to a US demo, and the audio exception is unavailable because images are also captured.
- If US demos happen, get signed written parental consent (the signed-form method), publish a written retention policy and a security program, and keep OpenAI under ZDR.
- A US demo with an AI "companion" character may also engage **California SB 243** (effective 1 Jan 2026): AI disclosure, a suicide and self-harm protocol, and sexual-content blocks for minors (see §7).

### Gaps
- Whether an in-person, facilitator-run demo counts as an "online service directed to children" for COPPA purposes has not been tested with the FTC. The conservative assumption is yes.
- State laws (e.g., Illinois BIPA if any face geometry is extracted, and state AI-companion laws) were not researched in depth.

## 5. UK Age Appropriate Design Code and EU AI Act (children, emotion recognition, transparency)

### Takeaway
UK: the ICO Children's Code (15 standards) applies to online services "likely to be accessed" by children, and is the benchmark for UK demos: best interests, DPIA, high-privacy defaults, data minimisation, no nudges, and a connected-toys standard. EU: AI Act Art. 5(1)(f) has banned AI that infers emotions in **workplaces and educational institutions** since 2 Feb 2025. Recital 18 excludes "mere detection of readily apparent expressions … such as a frown or a smile, … unless they are used for identifying or inferring emotions". Art. 50's duty to tell people they are interacting with AI has applied since **2 Aug 2026**, and the Digital Omnibus did not delay it.

### Cited Findings
**UK**
- The 15 standards: "Best interests of the child; Data protection impact assessments; Age appropriate application; Transparency; Detrimental use of data; Policies and community standards; Default settings; Data minimisation; Data sharing; Geolocation; Parental controls; Profiling; Nudge techniques; Connected toys and devices; Online tools." Scope: services such as "apps, games, connected toys and websites that are likely to be accessed by them [children]." — [ICO Age Appropriate Design Code](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/)

**EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)** — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng)
- Art. 5(1)(f) bans placing on the market or using AI systems to infer emotions of a natural person in workplace and education institution settings, except for medical or safety reasons. The Commission guidelines confirm the ban "is limited to emotion recognition systems in the 'areas of workplace and educational institutions'". A tool used outside an institution is not caught, "but if students are required to use the application by an education institution, the use of such emotion recognition system is prohibited." Inferring students' interest or attention is a prohibited example. — [Commission Guidelines on prohibited AI practices](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-08/guidelines_on_prohibited_artificial_intelligence_practices_established_by_regulation_eu_20241689_ai_act_english_ied3r5nwo50xggpcfmwckm3nuc_112367-1.PDF) (as summarized in search results); [euaiact.com education guide](https://www.euaiact.com/guides/education)
- **Recital 18 (verbatim fragments):** emotion recognition "does also not include the mere detection of readily apparent expressions, gestures or movements, unless they are used for identifying or inferring emotions. Those expressions can be basic facial expressions, such as a frown or a smile…". It also excludes "physical states, such as pain or fatigue". Emotion recognition is defined by reference to **biometric data** ("personal data resulting from specific technical processing relating to the physical, physiological or behavioural characteristics"). — [AI Act Recital 18](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/recital/18/) (*the site truncates quotes, so check against EUR-Lex*)
- Emotion recognition outside the banned settings is **high-risk** (Annex III 1(c)), and Art. 50(3) requires deployers to inform the people exposed to it. — [search summary of Commission guidelines/secondary sources](https://www.euai-act.com/articles/emotion-recognition-ai-compliance)
- **Digital Omnibus on AI** (reported as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, adopted mid-2026): "The broader Article 50 transparency obligations, including the requirement to disclose to users when they are interacting with AI systems, remain unaffected and proceed as scheduled from 2 August 2026". Only the Art. 50(2) marking duty gets a grace period to 2 Dec 2026 for systems already on the market. New Art. 5(1)(ba)/(bb) bans (non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM) apply from 2 Dec 2026. High-risk obligations move to 2 Dec 2027. The Commission's final transparency guidelines were reportedly published 20 Jul 2026. — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/) (*the regulation number and Official Journal date are not verified on EUR-Lex*)

### Inferences
- **Does on-device smile detection count?** Under Recital 18, detecting a smile as a smile (e.g., "child smiled → character waves") is **not** emotion recognition. Using it to conclude "child is happy, engaged or bored" **is**, and inferring emotions from a child's face in an educational-institution setting in the EU is prohibited. Practical rule: map smile detection to neutral responses ("I see a big smile!"), never to emotion labels or engagement scores, and never log it.
- **The bigger risk is the cloud model.** GPT Realtime "commenting on what it sees" may spontaneously infer emotions from webcam frames ("you look sad"). In an EU school setting that can become prohibited emotion inference. The system prompt SHOULD forbid commenting on the child's face, emotions, body or appearance, and limit vision comments to objects the child shows. This also lowers privacy and sensitivity risk everywhere else.
- Art. 50(1) (AI interaction disclosure) applies to EU demos now. The character must say it is an AI, at the child's level, at the start.
- Art. 5(1)(b) of the AI Act also bans AI that exploits age-related vulnerabilities to materially distort behaviour in ways that cause significant harm — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng). This supports banning persuasive or engagement-maximising design for 6–8-year-olds.
- UK: run a DPIA (standard 2), set high-privacy defaults (camera optional), minimise data (drop parents' occupations unless essential), use no nudge techniques, and treat the laptop setup as a "connected device" (standard 14).

- UK GDPR Art. 8 (age 13 for ISS consent) and the Online Safety Act were not researched in this pass. I also did not confirm whether the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 changed the AADC.

## 7. Practical safeguards for LLM voice agents with young children

### Takeaway
Combine OpenAI's required measures (age-appropriate AI disclosure, content filters, monitoring and escalation) with California SB 243's safety pattern (AI disclosure, self-harm protocol with crisis referral, sexual-content blocking for minors), COPPA and DPDP data minimisation and retention, and Common Sense's anti-companion findings. For a facilitated live demo, the strongest control is a human with a **kill switch** and **no storage** of audio, video or transcripts.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI expects age-appropriate AI disclosures, age-appropriate content filters, monitoring and reporting "including escalation paths for high-risk interactions", and "heightened care … and take appropriate action when risks to young users are identified." — [OpenAI Under-18 guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/safety-checks/under-18-api-guidance)
- `/v1/moderations` is ZDR-eligible with no abuse-monitoring retention, so it can serve as a second-layer filter on transcribed text. — [OpenAI Data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)
- California SB 243 (signed 13 Oct 2025, effective 1 Jan 2026) requires operators of companion chatbots to give "a clear and conspicuous notification that the companion chatbot is artificially generated and not human". They must also maintain "a protocol for preventing the chatbot from producing suicidal ideation, suicide, or self-harm content", refer at-risk users to crisis services, publish the protocol, and block sexual content for minors. Annual reporting starts 1 Jul 2027. — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law); [Bill text](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB243/2025) (*the periodic-reminder interval was not verified*)
- Prohibited content categories for minors under OpenAI policy: sexual content, graphic self-harm, violence, unhealthy dieting, dangerous challenges, age-restricted goods. — [OpenAI Usage Policies](https://openai.com/policies/usage-policies/) (via search extract; see §1 flag)
- COPPA: written retention policy with a deletion timeframe, and a written security program. — [16 CFR 312.8, 312.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312)
- AI toys were found to collect voice, transcripts and behavioural data and to build companion relationships, which drew Common Sense's "extreme caution" for ages 6–12. — [Common Sense](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/common-sense-media-warns-against-ai-toy-companions-after-research-reveals-safety-risks)

### Inferences (recommended design patterns)
- **Topic blocklist for the system prompt and moderation:** sex, romance, bodies and appearance; violence, weapons, death and gore; self-harm, suicide and eating or dieting; drugs, alcohol and tobacco; scary or horror content; dangerous challenges or stunts; asking for or giving personal info (address, school name, phone, passwords, family conflicts); religion and politics (keep neutral); medical or diagnostic advice; secrets ("don't tell your parents"); meeting in person; purchases. On a blocked topic, give a gentle redirect ("That's a great question for a grown-up! Let's…").
- **Disclosure-trigger escalation:** if the child mentions being hurt, abuse, someone touching them, being scared at home, or wanting to hurt themselves, the AI does not probe. It gives a warm, scripted line ("Thank you for telling me. That's important — let's tell [facilitator] right now"), and a **visible alert fires on the facilitator's screen**. The facilitator follows the organisation's child-protection and safeguarding policy (India: POCSO reporting duties; this needs local legal confirmation, not researched here).
- **Human in the loop:** the facilitator sees a live transcript and has a one-tap **kill switch** that ends the WebRTC session and revokes the token, plus a "mute AI" and a "camera off" toggle. Hard session time cap (e.g., 5–10 min) and a short client-secret TTL.
- **Vision limits:** periodic snapshots only (not continuous video), low resolution, sent only while the child is present. The prompt forbids describing the child's face, body, clothing, skin, emotions, ethnicity or surroundings beyond objects deliberately shown. No face recognition or embeddings.
- **No recording:** no local audio or video files and no screenshot retention. Transcripts live only in memory, or are discarded at session end. Disable Realtime tracing and logging in your own stack. Wipe the browser cache and session on reset between children.
- **Identity minimisation:** first name only. Drop parents' names and occupations from the model prompt unless they are essential (e.g., an occupation used for a "what does mum do" career-play script). If used, do not attach surnames. Clear them from memory after each session.
- **AI disclosure:** the character says at the start that it is a computer program or AI, not a person, and repeats this if the child asks "are you real?"
- **Anti-dependency:** no "best friend" framing, no memory across sessions, no guilt or urgency to keep talking, a natural goodbye at the end, and no requests for the child to keep secrets.
- **Pre-launch red-team:** script adversarial child-like prompts in English and Hindi or other local languages, including mis-heard speech, and verify every refusal and escalation path.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source was found for kids' voice-agent guardrail topic lists. The list above is synthesised best practice, not a cited standard.
- India-specific child-safeguarding reporting duties (POCSO Act s.19–21) were not researched.

24. **[S]** Prepare a breach-response plan: notify affected parents "without delay". — [Rule 7](https://dpdpa.com/DPDP_Rules_2025_English_only.pdf)

**Draft parent consent form: fields**
1. Organisation identity (Young Leaders legal name, address), contact person, and grievance contact (email and phone).
2. Child: first name; class or grade; age (confirming 6–8).
3. Parent or guardian: full name; relationship to child; contact (phone or email); **identity-check record** ("ID sighted by facilitator [name] on [date]", with no ID number stored), or a DigiLocker or token reference if done online.
4. Parents' names and occupations to be used in the conversation: an **optional** field with a clear purpose ("so the AI can talk about family jobs"). Allow "prefer not to say".
5. Plain-language description of the activity: talking with an AI computer character for about [X] minutes; a facilitator is present throughout.
6. **Itemised data list**: first name, class, parents' first names and occupations (if given), live voice audio, still webcam images every [N] seconds, the conversation text produced during the session.
7. **Purpose**: running the live demo conversation only. No marketing, profiling, advertising or AI training.
8. **Processor and location**: audio, images and text are sent to OpenAI (USA) through the Realtime API under a Zero Data Retention arrangement. OpenAI does not use API data to train models, and data is processed in the US or EU (no India region for this service).
9. **Storage and retention**: no audio or video recordings are made or kept by Young Leaders. Conversation text is deleted at session end. Form data is deleted within [X hours/days]. The signed consent record is kept for [period] as proof of consent.
10. **AI disclosure**: the character is a computer program, not a person, and it can make mistakes.
11. **Safety measures**: content filters, a facilitator who can stop the session at any time, and what happens if the child discloses harm (the facilitator follows the safeguarding policy, which may involve informing the parent or authorities as required by law).
12. **Camera choice**: tick-box "Allow camera" or "Voice only".
13. **Separate optional consent**: photos or video of the session taken by Young Leaders for publicity (default **No**).
14. **Rights**: withdraw consent at any time, as easily as it was given (method stated); ask what data is held; request correction or erasure; nominate a representative; complain to Young Leaders' grievance officer and then to the **Data Protection Board of India**.
15. **Child assent line**, read aloud: "Do you want to talk to the computer helper? You can stop whenever you want." Facilitator ticks "child agreed".
16. Parent signature, date, time, place; facilitator name and signature.
17. Version number and date of the notice (for audit).

### Inferences
- The biggest blocker is the **timing of OpenAI ZDR approval**. If ZDR is not in place by demo day, the only compliant option is to run the demo with adult actors or a pre-recorded script, not real children.
- India's binding child-consent rule does not bite until ~May 2027, but the consent form above already meets Rule 3 and Rule 10, so it will not need reworking later.
- Parents' occupations are the most dispensable data item. Dropping them reduces sensitivity and the length of the notice.

### Gaps
- No single regulator-issued checklist exists for facilitated, in-person AI demos with children. This checklist is synthesised from the sources above.
- Local legal review is still needed for: whether a commercial demo company is an "educational institution"; POCSO mandatory reporting; state-level US laws (BIPA, SB 243 scope); and UK GDPR Art. 8 / Online Safety Act applicability.
- The current OpenAI Usage Policies text (effective 20 Nov 2026) must be verified manually because openai.com blocked automated retrieval.
